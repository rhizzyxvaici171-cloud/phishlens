    What this checks for

    • Brand lookalikes and embedding, like paypa1.com or amazon-support.net, spelled to survive a quick glance.
    • The "@" trick, where everything before an unencoded @ is ignored by the browser, so the visible-looking destination isn't the real one.
    • Punycode and mixed-script domains, which can render as characters from a different alphabet that look identical to Latin letters.
    • Raw IP addresses, risky TLDs, and link shorteners, all common ways to dodge domain-based filtering or hide a real destination.

    This is a pattern check on the URL text itself, not a verdict. It doesn't visit the link, scan for malware, or check reputation databases. A clean result isn't a guarantee, and a flagged result isn't automatically dangerous, just worth a second look.
